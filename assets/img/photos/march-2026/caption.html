During the years that WWI and WWII spanned, more people died of Tuberculosis than in war. Today, more people die from TB than malaria, homicide, and war combined. Unlike during WWI and WWII, however, we can cure TB now. We can no longer claim it is a force of nature, now it is a force of injustice. TB should not be the deadliest infectious disease in the world but choose a world where it is.
This past week, myself and nearly 300 global health advocates, experts, and survivors (coming in from all 50 US States and a US territory!) met with every office in the senate and a large portion of the house. All three staffers I met with were open and supportive of our asks to restore funding. One staffer was even a TB survivor herself, it was touching hearing her personal story.
This past year, billions of dollars have been cut or unconstitutionally withheld from global health funding. It’s estimated that 500,000 to a million people died last year as a result. Every single one of those deaths is an outrage than cannot be understated. Even once we get the funding back, the systems have been damage. As former lead of global health at USAID, Atul Gawande, said on the idea of “paused” funding, “It’s like saying there’s a pause in a plane mid-flight.” You cannot pause it, you can only keep moving forward or you can crash it. There’s a lot of work to do and it will be hard but we won’t do it alone. We need everyone’s help.
